## Math {#math}


### Shapes of Inequalities {#shapes-of-inequalities}

<https://www.andreinc.net/2026/03/16/the-shape-of-inequalities/>
\\[
\frac{2}{\frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b}} \leq \sqrt{ab} \leq \frac{a + b}{2} \leq \sqrt{\frac{a^2 + b^2}{2}}
\\]
The first one is the **harmonic mean**, which is used to denote the average speed when going from point \\(A\\) at \\(v\_1\\) to point \\(B\\) at \\(v\_2\\). The actual average speed will then be \frac{$2}{\frac{1}{v\_1} + \frac{1}{v\_2}}$. This is called the harmonic mean because it is part of the very laws of our universe.

The second one is the **geometric mean** and is used to indicate growth. It is commonly used for compounding and scaling. So if a value increases by 100% and then 50%, the average growth is \\(\sqrt{2.0 \* 0.5} = 1.0\\).

The third one is the familiar **arithmetic mean**.

The last one is the **quadratic mean**, or the **root mean square**. This is used in electrical engineering. Remember phasors and complex equations?

### Snail's Teeth are Stronger Than Spider Silk {#snail-s-teeth-are-stronger-than-spider-silk}

<https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/spider-silk-loses-top-spot-natures-strongest-material-snails-teeth-180954346/>
Marine snails (limpets) can literally bite and break down rocks with a force of about **75 lbs per square inch**. This is done using their mollusk foot and a chemical secretion. It also turns out that the material is about **five times** stronger than most spider silk, and can withstand pressure which turns carbon into diamond. The teeth are made of goethite (iron-containing crystal) nanofibers encased in a protein matrix (sounds a lot like a composite!).

The strongest materials are still human-made, like graphene, but the snail's teeth is as good as kevlar. Currently the strongest materials are wurtzite boron nitrate and lonsdaleite.

### Early Alarm Clocks {#early-alarm-clocks}

<https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20260306-the-wake-up-tricks-people-used-before-alarm-clocks>
Lots of interesting techniques, using clocks which drop nails or burning incenses at one's feet. Of course there less unusual methods like listening to animal cues (like a rooster's call) or waiting on a stimulus like sunlight. There are also candles that clamp down and make a noise (via a marble or a literal clamping down) when the flame is out, which serves as an alarm clock (candle clocks).

The article does note that in parts of Europe, people would rise early to pray (because they believe it brings them closer to their God) which would mean animal cues may not be early enough. Later in the industrial era, they hired **knocker uppers** which are people they hired to stay up late and then wake people up early (like around 3 AM). They would make loud banging noises or shoot peas (Mary Smith was well known for this) to wake them up. Apparently whistles and bells were not sufficient for this, which makes sense, since I probably won't wake up to the sound of a bell either.

The article then makes an interesting point about whether they had a healthier sleep schedule, which people assumed was true. Interestingly enough, this may not be the case due to noisier crowds and physically demanding work (which can apparently affect sleep).


### New Sweden {#new-sweden}

<https://www.bbc.com/travel/article/20260629-new-sweden-the-uss-long-lost-secret-colony>
Apparently there was a Swedish colony started in the early 1600s (this is why Philadelphia has elements of the Swedish flag on their flags). They brought over the log cabin (the oldest cabin was here, called the CA Nothnagle Log House) and Lutherian Christianity. Funnily enough, the colony was started to spite the Dutch; Peter Minuit was not happy about his Netherland employers, who dismissed him. And as expected, the Dutch found out very quickly and considered them squatters, but did nothing due to lack of manpower. And so Fort Christina became the first permanent European settlement in the Delaware valley.

The colony was chronically understaffed, and Minuit drowned trying to find tobacco so it was actually just doom and gloom for the colony all year round. The Swedes survived due to the Natives, which they have a very favorable relationship with, especially since the Swedes were Forest Finns (Finland). Later **Johan Printz** (nicknamed Big Belly) who was **7ft tall and 400 pounds** came and created two more bastions near the river and expanded the colony from Cecil County, Maryland to Trenton, New Jersey. This was in addition to creating a new capital near Tinicum Island, Philadelphia.

Printz ruled with an iron fist (to make sure no one leaves the colony because it was understaffed and Sweden did not care about it), but was still favorable to the natives. Eventually the settlers had enough and signed a petition against him (a quarter of the settlers) and he **actually stepped down peacefully**. Then Peter Stuyvesant, Netherland Governor, finally had enough and sent seven armed ships to the settlement in 1655. Of course the Swedes surrendered, but the settlement was just absorbed, nothing else happened.
