Black powder purchased in the US are generally labeled with letters `F` and `g`. The `F` stands for "fine" and is a measure of how fine the powder is. More specifically, it indicates how small the grain sizes of the powder is.

| Powder Grade | Mesh Size | Average Size (mm)    |
|--------------|-----------|----------------------|
| Fg           | 12 mesh   | 1.7 mm (0.0661 in)   |
| FFg          | 16 mesh   | 1.18 mm (0.0469 in)  |
| FFFg         | 20 mesh   | 0.85 mm (0.0331 in)  |
| FFFFg        | 40 mesh   | 0.47 mm (0.0185 in)  |
| FFFFFg       | 75 mesh   | 0.149 mm (0.0058 in) |

The more `F` the powder has, the finer it is. Instead of FFFg, you can also say 3Fg. There are actually three other grades: whaling, cannon, saluting (A-1). These are all larger than Fg and are generally used for cannons. As its name suggests, A-1 is used in blanks for artillery to signal saluting. Note that the finer the powder, the faster it burns!
