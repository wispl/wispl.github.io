## Introduction {#introduction}

This section will include information on both Altimeter Sled and an Electronics Bay (AV Bay). We are going to use redundancy for the components. So for as many components as possible, use two of the same type, but from different manufacturers. The reason for different manufactures is to minimize issues due to manufacturing error. See [Flight Computer](@/notes/20260606203327-flight_computer.md) for more context.

When using an altimeter, you are going to need to poke three or four holes somewhere in the payload bay for the altimeters, see [Altimeter Hole Sizes](@/notes/20260607211817-altimeter_hole_sizes.md). For arming the rocket there are a couple of choices

-   contact switches and pull pin
-   key switches
-   slide switches
-   magnetic switches
-   remote activated switches

In short, there are four main sections you need to worry about

1.  flight computers and components: your devices that gather data and perform staging
2.  wiring: how things are wired between each other
3.  arming: how to activate the electronics
4.  staging: how the parachute is deployed

Arming is particularly important because it is probably a really bad idea to walk or drive anywhere with an armed rocket. Since we are using electronics to drive recovery, we need to use [Recovery Charges](@/notes/20260704195517-recovery_charges.md) (like ejection charges, but not from a motor).


## Altimeter Sled {#altimeter-sled}


### Components {#components}

The simpler Electronic Bay, used for [Cable Cutter Dual Deploy](@/notes/20260603030110-cable_cutter_dual_deploy.md).

-   Two altimeters (RRC2+ or RRC3 and a Featherweight Raven are good choices)
-   Two cable cutters
-   Igniters (you need two, but get extra for testing ejection charges)
-   Two 9-volt batteries (Duracaell) and battery connectors
-   22 gauge hook up wires (different colors for labelling is nice)
-   Radio transmitter

You can place the sled in either the hollow nose cone or in the payload bay. It is recommended to mount the batteries near the bottom of the payload bay. This prevents the battery from falling and pulling everything else as it falls. Make sure the battery is well secured as well.


### Wiring {#wiring}

Also make sure to wire the batteries, switches and computers for redundancy. That is each pair is independent, don't use one battery for both computers or use one switch for both batteries. Each altimeter should get its own switch and battery.

You can use other gauge wire, but 22 gauge seems to be a happy medium.

-   copper is more expensive but has less resistance than aluminum
-   solid wire is easier to use than stranded but stranded is more resilient to bending, use stranded for altimeter to igniter connection

-   single piece of wire surrounded by an insulator
-   many small wires bundled and surrounded by an insulator

You can use terminal blocks to bridge wires from inside the sled to ones outside the sled. Make sure to cut a hole on the bottom plate of the bulkhead though which the wires inside can cross out to reach the terminal blocks. These wires are used for the altimeter to igniter connection.

When connection wires into the terminal,

1.  cut around a small bit of insulation from the wires
2.  apply tinning (twist strands into a solid bundled and then apply a little bit of solder)

This makes the wire to terminal block connection more secure so that a stray wire does not slip out and touch the other terminals. If you use this method, you need to fill up the hole where the wires come out of (using epoxy maybe). This ensures the ejection gases don't leak into the bay where the altimeters sleep.


### Arming {#arming}

Key switches are good, but they need to properly mounted to the the rocket, otherwise they will turn with the key, rendering them useless.

Contact switches with a pull pin is another popular idea. The pin can have a "REMOVE BEFORE FLIGHT" tag to make it really visible. For this, you need a switch for each battery.


### Staging {#staging}

In this case we are doing a wire cutter. This part is really dependent on how your rocket looks (hollow nose cone or payload bay). In the case of the payload bay, you would usually have

1.  Nose cone
2.  Payload bay
3.  Parachute
4.  Rest of rocket

So you would need to feed wires from the payload bay to the parachute area. Recall the ignition area should be isolated from your electronics (sealed off) so the gases won't mess up the altimeters.

To do this, you could use terminal blocks, as mentioned before.


## Electronics Bay {#electronics-bay}

This one is for [Two Parachute Dual Deploy](@/notes/20260603030122-two_parachute_dual_deploy.md) and is a little bit more complex. The wiring, arming, and components are the same. The difference is that the payload bay is between two parachutes, so you need to wire both ends. And of course no wire cutters are used.
